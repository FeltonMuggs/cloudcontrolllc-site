# FoMGL Sponsorship — Call Scripts & Objection Handling

For the cold call, the voicemail, and the 15-minute discovery call that outbound
is trying to book. Scripts are scaffolding, not lines to read — the discovery
questions matter more than the pitch.

## Cold call opener

Permission-based. It beats the enthusiastic version with executives.

> "[FIRST_NAME], this is [SENDER_NAME] with the Government Blockchain Association.
> I know I'm calling out of the blue — can I have 30 seconds to say why, and then
> you can tell me to go away?"
>
> *(pause — wait for the yes)*
>
> "We run FoMGL, our annual conference on money, government and law. It is
> [EVENT_DATES] in [VENUE_CITY], and the room is about [ATTENDEE_COUNT] people,
> roughly [GOV_ATTENDEE_PCT] of them from government, across [AGENCY_COUNT]
> agencies.
>
> I called [COMPANY] specifically because of [SPECIFIC_PROOF]. Companies doing
> that kind of work usually tell me their problem is not the product, it is
> getting in front of anyone who can actually sponsor a program.
>
> Is that fair, or have you solved that already?"

Then stop talking. The answer determines everything after.

## Voicemail — under 20 seconds

> "[FIRST_NAME], [SENDER_NAME] with the Government Blockchain Association,
> [SENDER_PHONE].
>
> Calling about FoMGL [EVENT_YEAR] and [COMPANY]'s [SECTOR] work specifically —
> not a general blast. Sending an email now with the same thing so you can ignore
> whichever is more convenient. [SENDER_PHONE]."

Always send the email within five minutes of leaving the voicemail. The pair
converts far better than either alone.

## Discovery call — 15 minutes

Structure: 2 minutes framing, 8 minutes questions, 3 minutes recommendation, 2
minutes next step. If you are talking more than half the call, it is going badly.

### Frame it

> "Here is what I would like to do with the 15 minutes: understand who you are
> actually trying to reach next year, and then tell you honestly whether FoMGL
> gets you in front of them. If it doesn't, I would rather say so — I would
> rather have you back in a future year than sell you the wrong thing now."

### The eight questions

Ask these in order. The answers write the proposal.

1. "Who are you trying to sell to in [EVENT_YEAR] — which agencies, which level,
   federal or state or international?"
2. "How are you reaching them today, and how is that working?"
3. "What's the pursuit that would change your year if it landed?"
4. "Have you sponsored events like this before? What happened?"
5. "When you evaluate a sponsorship afterward, what makes it a yes to do again —
   meetings, pipeline, brand, something else?"
6. "Who besides you weighs in on a decision like this?"
7. "What does the budget cycle look like — is there money now, or does this need
   to wait for [NEXT_PERIOD]?"
8. "If we get this right, what does the week after the conference look like for
   your team?"

### Recommend, do not present

Never walk all `[TIER_COUNT]` tiers. Name one, with a fallback.

> "Based on what you said about [THEIR_STATED_GOAL], the one that fits is
> `[TIER_X_NAME]` at `[TIER_X_PRICE]`. The reason is [BENEFIT_1] and [BENEFIT_2]
> — those map directly to [WHAT_THEY_SAID].
>
> The tier below is `[TIER_Y_NAME]` at `[TIER_Y_PRICE]`. It gets you in the room
> but not on the program, which given what you told me about [GOAL] I think
> undersells what you are trying to do. Your call.
>
> What is your reaction to that number?"

### Close on the next step, not the signature

> "Two ways to go. I can send the agreement today and hold `[TIER_X_NAME]` until
> `[HOLD_DEADLINE]`, or if [OTHER_STAKEHOLDER] needs to weigh in, I can put the
> package in writing for you to take to them and we reconnect [SPECIFIC_DAY].
>
> Which is more useful?"

Always leave with a date on the calendar. "I'll follow up next week" is how
deals die.

## Objection handling

Acknowledge, reframe, ask. Never argue.

**"We don't have budget for this."**
> "Understood, and that is a real constraint. Two things worth separating: is
> there no budget at all this cycle, or no budget at `[TIER_1_PRICE]`? Most
> first-time sponsors start at `[TIER_4_NAME]` around `[TIER_4_PRICE]`. And if
> the answer is genuinely nothing this year, tell me when the [YEAR] planning
> happens and I will come back then rather than pushing now."

**"We already do our own events / dinners."**
> "That makes sense, and those usually work — for people who already know you.
> The gap is the officials who have never heard of [COMPANY] and will not accept
> a dinner invitation from a vendor they do not know. That is the group FoMGL
> convenes. It is additive to what you are doing, not a replacement."

**"How do we know we'll get ROI?"**
> "You do not, and I would not trust anyone who promised it. What I can tell you
> is who is in the room and what access the tier buys — that is in the prospectus
> and it is verifiable. What I would rather do is define the measure now: if you
> tell me what a successful outcome looks like, we can build the package around
> it and design the follow-up so you can actually measure it afterward. What
> number would make this a yes to repeat?"

**"Send me the information and I'll review it."**
> "Happy to. So I send something useful rather than a 20-page deck — is the
> question about which tier, about the audience, or whether this is the right
> year at all? Whichever it is, I will send that part and skip the rest."

**"We need to see the attendee list first."**
> "We do not share the attendee list — the government officials who attend are the
> reason for that, and the confidentiality is what keeps them coming. What I can
> share is the breakdown by agency type and seniority, and last year's session
> topics, which tells you the same thing. Would that answer it?"

**"Isn't this just pay-to-play?"**
> "Sponsorship buys presence and visibility. It does not buy a procurement
> decision, and any conference claiming otherwise is lying to you. What it buys is
> being in the room and on the program when these conversations happen — which
> matters precisely because you cannot buy the decision."

**"We're already a GBA member — isn't that enough?"**
> "Membership gets you the network, the working groups, and the credential.
> Sponsorship is the concentrated version: a specific audience, in a specific
> room, on a specific date, with your name on the program. Members do get
> `[MEMBER_RATE_NOTE]`, so the incremental cost is smaller than it looks."

**"Let me talk to my team and get back to you."**
> "Of course. Two things that would help: what will they want to know that I have
> not covered, and when do you think you will have talked? I would rather put a
> date on the calendar now than chase you — and if the answer comes back no, a
> one-line email is completely fine."

**"We sponsored before and it didn't work."**
> "Then I want to know what happened before I ask for anything. What did you
> expect and what did you get? [Listen fully.] If that was [BOOTH_PLACEMENT /
> WRONG_TIER / NO_FOLLOW_UP], here is specifically what would be different — and
> if I cannot make it different, I will tell you that too."

## After every call

Within 30 minutes, log to JoMoCo per `crm-workflow.md`: the pursuit, the stated
success measure, the other stakeholders, the budget timing, the objection raised,
and the committed next step with its date. Send the recap email the same day —
it is the single highest-leverage 10 minutes in the whole process.
