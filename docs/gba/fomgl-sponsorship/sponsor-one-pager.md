## The full one-pager

### Why FoMGL

The hardest part of selling blockchain into government is not the technology. It
is reaching the people who can sponsor a program.

Those people do not answer cold email. They are not reachable through a
procurement portal until a requirement already exists — by which point the
specification has usually been shaped by someone else. They do attend FoMGL,
because it is where the money, government, and law conversation actually happens.

FoMGL is convened by the Government Blockchain Association, whose network spans
500+ government offices and 50+ working groups where agency staff and industry
set standards together. That is the network sponsorship plugs you into.

### Add-ons

Sold separately, and worth raising on every proposal — attach rate on these is
the difference between an average and a strong campaign.

- Welcome reception or networking break sponsorship
- Delegation or pavilion sponsorship — for chapters and national delegations
- Workshop or side-session host
- Attendee bag, lanyard, badge, or Wi-Fi naming
- Awards category sponsorship
- Post-event press distribution via GBA News Hub

### Beyond the conference

Sponsorship works best as part of a government go-to-market program, not a
one-off. Ask about pairing it with:

- **GBA organizational membership** — year-round network and working group access
- **BMM certification** — the Blockchain Maturity Model assessment government
  buyers use as a procurement screen; details at
  https://www.gbaglobal.org/blockchain-maturity-model/
- **Capitol Hill Blockchain Showcase** — for solutions specifically targeting
  U.S. federal procurement

## Writing rules for every version

- Lead with the audience. The audience is the product; the tiers are packaging.
- Never claim procurement outcomes. Sponsorship buys presence, not decisions.
- Every number carries its source. Unsourced numbers get cut, not softened.
- Name the deadline only when it is real.
