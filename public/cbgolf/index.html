Executive Briefing · Strategic Land-Use & Public Infrastructure Assessment

4780 James Monroe Highway

Strategic repurposing as a dual-utility community golf amenity and municipal green infrastructure hub

Prepared for:Town of Colonial Beach Planning Commission, Town Administration & Department of Public Works Subject property:4780 James Monroe Highway, Colonial Beach, VA 22443 — Westmoreland County Tax Parcels 6-133, 6-133E, 6-133F

The transition of the longtime Colonial Beach Moose Lodge property gives the Town a chance to pair community recreation with pressing municipal infrastructure needs. Treated as an integrated public-private green infrastructure facility rather than a stand-alone commercial venture, the site can (1) offer a potential reclaimed-water reuse outlet as the Town upgrades its wastewater plant under its DEQ Consent Order, (2) act as a regional stormwater catchment that protects Freeneck Gut, and (3) deliver a family-oriented golf practice destination aligned with the 2030 Comprehensive Plan.

3Water Reuse & Water Supply

Concept only. Level 1 requires filtration and higher-level disinfection beyond secondary treatment; whether the Town plant can produce it is not yet confirmed.

DEQ Consent Order. After DEQ Notices of Violation for plant operations and spills, the Town negotiated a Consent Order (VPDES VA0026409, 2024). It requires a comprehensive facility evaluation, an effluent limit and overflow prevention plan, and funding commitments; its Supplemental Environmental Project is two years of bacteria monitoring. Water reuse is not part of the order. This site is offered as an option the Town could evaluate in its facility planning — not as a compliance measure.

Groundwater protection. The Town's drinking water comes from groundwater wells, and DEQ is urging eastern Virginia localities to reduce reliance on the declining Potomac Aquifer. Irrigating with reclaimed water or harvested stormwater instead of well water conserves that supply.

6Permitting Roadmap

  1. Jurisdiction & zoning — confirm Town vs. County authority; A-1 parcels need a Special Exception, CUP, or rezoning.
  2. Environmental site plan — delineate RPA buffer and FEMA AE/VE zones; keep structures on the ~3 upland acres.
  3. DEQ reuse feasibility — confirm the plant can meet Level 1; irrigation over 5 acres is “bulk” reuse, which may require a Nutrient Management Plan by a DCR-certified planner depending on the water's nutrient content; public-access sites need an education and notification program.
  4. Public-private MOU — effluent conveyance, maintenance, and public access hours for residents and schools.

5Phased Development Framework

9-hole putting course, chipping green with bunker, 30 & 55-yd pitching lanes, hitting bays or simulator cage, Drive, Chip & Putt youth lawn. No netting, no flood zone, parking and pavilion unchanged.

Phase 2 · Practice Facility + Infrastructure

Parcel 6-133F and adjacent clearings
140–160-yd limited-flight range, weather-station-controlled irrigation, dual-purpose stormwater retention wetlands.

Phase 3 · Regional Range + Full Reuse

Full acreage · long term
300-yd regulation range with containment netting and a dedicated non-potable line tied into Public Works.
A well-planned recreational landscape can work as living municipal infrastructure — protecting water supply, buffering storms, and turning public-works needs into a community amenity.
